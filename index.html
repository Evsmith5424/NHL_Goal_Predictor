Correlation adjustments

Only moderate or strong correlations (|r| of 0.40 or more, and statistically significant) are allowed to change the numbers. Measured on 2025-26, 41 home and 41 road games per team.

RelationshiprStrengthWhat the model does with it

Regress offense to the mean. Teams' scoring at home and on the road correlates at r = 0.53. Treating that as a split-half reliability and scaling it to a full season (Spearman-Brown: 2r ÷ (1 + r)) says about of a team's scoring edge over league average is repeatable skill. The rest is treated as luck, so each attack rating is pulled that far back toward average. Defense isn't regressed because its home/road correlation (0.38) is below the cutoff.

Home-ice edge. This one comes from a significant difference in averages rather than a correlation: home teams scored 3.15 a game, road teams 3.01 (). It only applies when you pick a home team. Because team strength carries across venues (r = 0.56), the same league-wide factor is used for every team instead of each team's own split.

How the model works

League average is goals per team per game. Each team gets an attack rating (its goals per game ÷ league average) and a defense rating (its goals allowed per game ÷ league average).

Team 1 expected goals = Team 1 attack × Team 2 defense × league average, and the same the other way. Goals are modeled as Poisson-distributed, which treats each scoring chance as independent. Total goals is also Poisson, with its expected value equal to the two teams' expected goals added together.

Limits: it uses season averages, uses the same home-ice factor for every team, ignores goalies, injuries and roster changes, and covers regulation only. Empty-net goals are part of the averages but not modeled separately. "Or more" columns are the chance of at least that many goals.
